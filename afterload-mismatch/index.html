🔴 Critical Insight — SvO₂ 47%
SvO₂ <60% = body extracting excess oxygen because delivery is failing. When CI is low, tissues compensate by pulling more O₂ from each RBC. Normal SvO₂ is 65–75%. At 47%, this patient is in high-extraction, low-output state — the definition of LCOS. This is not a fluid problem.
🫀 Pathophysiology — Why This Happens After AVR for Severe AS
1
Pre-op: LV hypertrophies over years to push against a calcified, tight aortic valve. High wall stress → concentric hypertrophy. EF preserved but muscle is stiff.
2
Valve replaced — obstruction gone. But the thick, non-compliant myocardium doesn't "know" the obstruction is removed. It still relaxes poorly (diastolic dysfunction) and needs high filling pressures to fill.
3
Afterload mismatch exposed: Pre-op, the high transvalvular gradient masked how much SVR was contributing. With the valve open, even normal SVR now impairs LV ejection. The stiff LV fails to generate adequate stroke volume.
4
Post-bypass: Add cardioplegia effect, SIRS, and the LV is temporarily stunned. Result = CI ↓, filling pressures ↑, SvO₂ ↓. High SVR is the body compensating — don't mistake it for a problem to fix with pressors.

✅ Management Priorities — LCOS After AVR
💊
Inotropic Support Milrinone preferred — inovasodilator reduces afterload AND improves contractility simultaneously
💧
Cautious Fluids Small boluses if CVP <8. Avoid flooding a stiff LV — high PCWP = pulmonary edema risk
⏱️
Heart Rate 60–80 Stiff LV needs time to fill. Tachycardia shortens diastole → inadequate filling → CI drops further
📉
Afterload Reduction NTG/nicardipine helps stiff LV empty. Lower SVR = better SV. This patient's NTG is appropriate
🔋
Pacing Wires Ready A&V epicardial wires placed intraop. A-fib history + Maze = monitor for bradycardia, heart block
📊
Trend SvO₂ + Lactate SvO₂ rising toward >60%, lactate clearing = LCOS resolving. These are your recovery markers
⚖ Clinical Decision Framework
✅ DO THIS
✓ Monitor SvO₂ trend — it tells you if CI is improving
✓ Use milrinone or dobutamine early if CI <2.0 persists
✓ Control HR 60–80; prioritize AV synchrony via pacing wires
✓ Reduce afterload (NTG, nicardipine) to help LV unload
✓ Call surgeon if CI <1.8 despite optimization
✗ AVOID THIS
✗ Don't reflexively fluid-bolus for low CI — check PCWP first
✗ Don't escalate vasopressors alone — high SVR worsens LV ejection
✗ Don't allow tachycardia >100 — it's lethal for a stiff LV
✗ Don't normalize SvO₂ by increasing FiO₂ — treat the CI
✗ Don't wait too long — rising lactate = escalate MCS discussion
🔬 Clinical Correlation — Numbers vs. Patient: This Case in Context
⚠ What the Numbers Suggest
CI 1.8 L/min/m² Below threshold for adequate output. Raises concern for LCOS — LV struggling to eject.
SvO₂ 47% High O₂ extraction suggests tissues aren't receiving adequate delivery — classic LCOS signature.
MAP 62 + PAP elevated Low-normal MAP with elevated filling pressures fits a pump-failure pattern on paper.
✅ What the Clinical Exam Shows
Good Urine Output Kidneys are among the first organs to sense low perfusion. Adequate UOP is a reliable real-time marker of organ-level CO delivery.
No Rising Lactate Lactate reflects anaerobic metabolism. A stable or clearing lactate means tissues are not in oxygen debt despite the low SvO₂ value.
Clinical Picture Otherwise Stable Numbers don't perfuse patients — organs do. When the clinical exam doesn't match the hemodynamic concern, investigate before intervening.
📋 Due Diligence Checklist
○ Lactate — rising, stable, or clearing? Rising = true LCOS. Stable/clearing = reassuring.
○ SvO₂ context — is patient shivering, febrile, or agitated? Increased O₂ demand pulls SvO₂ down without true delivery failure.
○ Anemia check — low Hgb = fewer O₂ carriers. Tissues extract more from each RBC → SvO₂ drops without low CO.
○ CI measurement validity — clean thermodilution runs? Arrhythmia, TR, or poor technique skew CI artificially low.
○ Extremities warm or cool? Warm + good UOP = compensated. Cool + mottled = vasoconstricting for low output.
○ Trend over 30–60 min — one snapshot misleads. Are numbers improving, worsening, or holding?
⚖️
The clinical lesson: Numbers trigger vigilance. The exam confirms or refutes. Good UOP in a warm, non-acidotic patient with stable lactate should reassure you — but CI 1.8 and SvO₂ 47% still demand systematic evaluation before dismissing them. Rule it out. Don't assume it out.
💡
Bedside Pearl — The Core Concept
"The valve is fixed, but the heart doesn't know that yet. It's thick, stiff, and now exposed to afterload it couldn't fully feel before. A high SVR means the body is compensating for low output — don't reward it with more vasopressors. Support the pump, reduce the load, and give the LV time to remodel."
