CVICU Arterial Line Accuracy Decision Guide

Evidence-Based Bedside Reference for Critical Care Nurses

📊 Waveform Analysis & Square Wave Test

Normal Waveform

Square Wave Test: Sharp upstroke, 1-2 oscillations, returns to baseline

Trust this reading: Accurate systolic, diastolic, and MAP

Underdamped Waveform

Square Wave Test: Multiple oscillations (>3), slow return to baseline

Caution: Systolic falsely high, diastolic falsely low. MAP usually accurate.

Overdamped Waveform

Square Wave Test: Slow upstroke, no oscillations, gradual return

Problem: Systolic falsely low, diastolic falsely high. Verify with cuff.

🌳 Clinical Decision Flowchart

A-line reading seems inaccurate
Step 1: Perform Square Wave Test
Normal waveform → Trust all readings
Underdamped → Trust MAP, verify SBP with cuff if critical
Overdamped → Verify with cuff, troubleshoot system
Step 2: Check System Components
✓ All connections tight
✓ No air bubbles in tubing
✓ Transducer at phlebostatic axis
✓ Flush bag >300 mmHg
Step 3: Clinical Correlation
Compare with patient's clinical picture
Consider recent changes (positioning, pressors, fluid status)
When in doubt: Manual cuff verification

🔧 Tubing Length & Compliance Effects

Tubing Factor Effect on Waveform Clinical Impact Solution
Long tubing (>4 feet) Underdamping, artifacts Falsely high SBP, low DBP Use shortest tubing possible
Compliant tubing Overdamping Falsely low SBP, high DBP Use non-compliant tubing
Air bubbles Severe overdamping Unreliable all pressures Remove all air, fast flush
Kinked tubing Dampened, irregular Variable accuracy Straighten, secure tubing

⚡ "Whip" Artifact Recognition

What is Whip Artifact?

High-frequency oscillations superimposed on the arterial waveform, causing falsely elevated systolic readings.

Common Causes

  • Patient movement/agitation
  • Ventilator synchrony issues
  • Catheter tip against vessel wall
  • High-compliance tubing
  • Excessive catheter length

Why MAP Remains Reliable

MAP is calculated as an average over time, so high-frequency artifacts are filtered out mathematically. The area under the curve remains accurate even with whip artifact.

Clinical Pearl: When SBP seems too high but patient appears stable, check MAP and DBP for clinical decisions.

🏥 CVICU-Specific Clinical Scenarios

Post-CABG Patient on Pressors

Situation: Post-op day 1, on norepinephrine, MAP goal >65

A-line shows: SBP 180/90, MAP 95 (seems underdamped)

Action: Trust MAP for pressor titration, but verify SBP with cuff if concerned about hypertension management

Consider: New onset A-fib can cause waveform irregularities

Post-Valve Surgery, Tight BP Control

Situation: Aortic valve replacement, SBP goal <140

A-line shows: Dampened waveform, SBP 110/85

Action: High suspicion for inaccuracy - verify with cuff before holding antihypertensives

Critical: Uncontrolled hypertension post-valve surgery can cause bleeding

IABP Patient Monitoring

Situation: Intra-aortic balloon pump in situ

A-line shows: Augmented diastolic pressures

Action: Focus on unassisted systolic pressure and MAP. Diastolic will be artificially elevated during balloon inflation

IABP timing affects all pressure readings - coordinate with perfusionist

💬 Communication & Escalation Guidelines

When to Verify with Cuff

  • Overdamped waveform
  • SBP >200 or <90 unexpectedly
  • Change >30 mmHg from baseline
  • Clinical picture doesn't match
  • Before medication adjustments

Reporting to Providers

SBAR Format:

S: "A-line reading seems inaccurate"

B: "Square wave test shows [finding]"

A: "Cuff pressure is [X], suggests [over/under]damping"

R: "Should we troubleshoot line or use cuff for now?"

Handoff Communication

  • "A-line reliability: Normal/Underdamped/Overdamped"
  • "Last cuff verification: [time] [value]"
  • "Using MAP for pressor titration"
  • "System troubleshot at [time]"
Red Flags - Immediate Provider Notification:
  • Unable to obtain reliable readings despite troubleshooting
  • Significant discrepancy (>30 mmHg) between A-line and cuff
  • Sudden loss of waveform with clinical deterioration
  • Signs of arterial compromise (pale, cool extremity)

📋 Quick Reference Checklist

Daily A-line Assessment

  • ☐ Square wave test performed
  • ☐ Waveform quality documented
  • ☐ Transducer level verified
  • ☐ System zeroed per protocol
  • ☐ Tubing assessed for bubbles
  • ☐ Insertion site evaluated

Troubleshooting Steps

  1. Perform square wave test
  2. Check transducer level
  3. Flush system, check for bubbles
  4. Verify all connections
  5. Compare with cuff pressure
  6. Document findings
  7. Notify provider if unresolved
