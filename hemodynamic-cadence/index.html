The Hemodynamic Cadence of Multi-Drug Support

Phenotype-first escalation, complementary drug pairing, and pharmacokinetic-aware de-escalation for adults with low cardiac reserve.

START HERE

Open the right hemodynamic pouch before choosing a drug

Medication choice follows physiology. A normal MAP can conceal low forward flow; a low MAP can coexist with adequate CI.

PRELOAD · How full is the tank?

Read: CVP, PA diastolic/PCWP, echo, PLR and SV response.

Tools: cautious volume, diuresis, venodilation and mechanical diagnosis.

CONTRACTILITY · How hard does it squeeze?

Read: CI/SV, SvO₂, CPO, lactate phenotype and echo.

Tools: dobutamine, low-dose epinephrine, milrinone, pacing and MCS evaluation.

AFTERLOAD · How tight is the nozzle?

Read: SVR/PVR, MAP/DBP and ventricular response.

Tools: NE/vasopressin when too loose; NTG/nicardipine/clevidipine when too tight.

SIX-QUESTION BEDSIDE SCAN

  1. Is CI/SV or CPO low—and is the measurement trustworthy?
  2. Is HR/rhythm helping or stealing filling time?
  3. Is preload inadequate, adequate, or congested?
  4. Is contractility failing despite adequate filling?
  5. Is systemic or pulmonary afterload mismatched?
  6. Could bleeding, tamponade, tension, ischemia, graft or device failure explain non-response?

NEVER TITRATE TO MAP ALONE

  • Flow: CI, SV, CPO, SvO₂
  • Coronary pressure: DBP and CPP
  • Congestion: CVP/PCWP and echo
  • Toxicity: HR, ectopy, ischemia, lactate phenotype
  • Perfusion: mentation, skin, urine and acid-base trend
ALGORITHM 1

Escalate by ceiling-and-pivot—not by maximizing one drug

Proposed pilot ranges require a current order set, clinician assessment and institutional policy.

LOW OUTPUT / STUNNED LV · CI <2.0, SvO₂ <60%, FILLING ADEQUATE/HIGH

1

Initial inotropy

Dobutamine 2–2.5 mcg/kg/min or epinephrine 0.01–0.02 mcg/kg/min. Reassess q15–20 or q10–15 min.

2

Ceiling

HR >95 or >10–15% above baseline, new ectopy/PVCs, dobutamine ~5, or epi 0.04–0.05 → cap drug A.

3

Complementary pivot

Consider milrinone 0.125–0.25 mcg/kg/min; omit loading bolus in unstable hypotension. Expect a 6–12 h build, longer in AKI.

DBP <50 or CPP <35: hold and verify with MD/APP; protect pressure with ordered NE and/or vasopressin.

POST-CPB VASOPLEGIA · MAP <65, SVR <800, CI PRESERVED/ADEQUATE

1

Adrenergic tone

NE 0.02–0.04 mcg/kg/min; reassess q5–10 min.

2

Ceiling pivot

NE ~0.08–0.10 or SVR >1100 with falling CI → cap NE; add vasopressin 0.03 U/min under active order.

3

Allow the slower component to declare itself

Assess the combined response over ~45–60 min before repeatedly stacking dose.

Refractory vasoplegia/acidosis: notify MD/APP for rescue evaluation.

RV FAILURE / HIGH PVR · RISING CVP, RV DILATION, PULMONARY HYPERTENSION

1

Correct PVR drivers

Hypoxia, acidosis, temperature, rhythm/AV synchrony and preload without RV overdistension.

2

Protect RV perfusion

Ordered NE ± low-dose vasopressin to preserve DBP/CPP.

3

Uncouple the RV

Milrinone 0.125–0.25 without bolus and/or inhaled pulmonary vasodilator under MD/APP direction.

AFTERLOAD EXCESS / HYPERTENSIVE LOW OUTPUT

1

Choose the vascular target

Nitroglycerin for preload/ischemia/conduit spasm; nicardipine or clevidipine for arterial afterload.

2

Pair when flow remains low

Combine afterload reduction with ordered dobutamine/milrinone rather than escalating catecholamine alone.

3

Match cadence

Clevidipine is minute-to-minute; NTG is rapid; nicardipine accumulates more slowly and has a longer tail. Reassess CI/SV—not BP alone.

ALGORITHM 2

PK-aware de-escalation: remove the liability, preserve the bridge

Enter only after a stable trend, correction of reversible causes, and exclusion of bleeding or mechanical failure.

1

Remove epinephrine liability

0.04 → 0.02 → 0.01 → off, approximately q15–20 min.

2

Trim norepinephrine to a low buffer

Reduce toward 0.02–0.04 mcg/kg/min, q10–15 min. HR/CI may fall as β₁ support disappears.

3

Test vasopressin while retaining a small NE rescue path

When NE ≤0.04: 0.03 → 0.02 → 0.01 → off, q45–60 min. Sequence is phenotype-dependent; comparative evidence is not universal.

4

Remove residual norepinephrine

0.02 → off, q10–15 min if DBP/CPP and forward flow remain stable.

5

Wean inotrope / inodilator

Dobutamine 5 → 2.5 → 1 → off, q20–30 min. Milrinone 0.25 → 0.1875 → 0.125 → off, generally q6–8 h; consider q12–24 h in significant AKI/CRRT.

THE MILRINONE–PRESSOR BRIDGE

  1. Reduce milrinone by the ordered step.
  2. Keep low NE and/or vasopressin during the residual vasodilatory tail.
  3. Wait hours—not minutes; longer with renal dysfunction.
  4. Reassess DBP/CPP, CI/SV, filling pressure, SvO₂ and perfusion.
  5. Only then test removal of the short-acting pressure bridge.

Inference: pharmacologically coherent and supported by milrinone PK/vasodilation, but the exact bridge sequence requires prospective validation.

HOLD WEANING; VERIFY WITH MD/APP

  • Meaningful DBP/MAP/CPP deterioration
  • CI/SV/CPO or SvO₂ deterioration from the stable trend
  • CVP/PCWP rise, RV dilation or pulmonary edema
  • New ischemia, tachyarrhythmia or ectopy
  • Unexplained lactate rise, oliguria, cool skin, altered mentation or acidosis

AFTERLOAD REDUCERS ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE

Nitroglycerin
Best for venous preload reduction, ischemia or conduit spasm; can collapse preload in an underfilled or RV-dependent patient.

Nicardipine
Sustained arterial unloading; avoid rapid stacking before its effect declares itself.

Clevidipine
Ultra-short arterial control when frequent fine adjustment and reversibility matter.

PATTERN RECOGNITION

Combination scenarios: why they fit, how they fail, what to reassess

Teaching scenarios—not prescriptive orders.

01

Stunned LV + vasodilation

Combination · Dobutamine or low-dose epi + NE

Why · Flow support plus DBP/CPP protection.

Failure · Tachycardia or rising afterload with falling SV.

Pivot · Cap catecholamine; consider milrinone while preserving a pressure bridge.

02

Tachycardia-limited inotropy

Combination · Milrinone + low-dose NE or vasopressin

Why · Non-β inotropy/lusitropy while countering dilation.

Failure · Pressor removed before milrinone is reduced or cleared.

Pivot · Use the PK bridge; wait longer in AKI.

03

Mixed cardiogenic–vasoplegic shock

Combination · Inotrope/inodilator + NE + vasopressin

Why · Separates flow from tone and limits single-drug dose.

Failure · MAP improves while CI/SV falls from excess afterload.

Pivot · Trend CPO, SvO₂ and filling pressure with SVR.

04

Isolated RV failure / high PVR

Combination · Milrinone + NE ± vasopressin; inhaled pulmonary vasodilator

Why · Reduces PVR while preserving right-coronary pressure.

Failure · Systemic pressure loss or RV overdistension.

Pivot · Protect DBP; correct hypoxia, acidosis and rhythm.

05

Post-CPB vasoplegia with adequate CI

Combination · NE → add vasopressin

Why · Adrenergic plus V1a tone; catecholamine-sparing.

Failure · Persistent acidosis or rising SVR with declining CI.

Pivot · Stop stacking and notify for rescue evaluation.

06

Hypertensive low-output LV

Combination · Dobutamine/milrinone + clevidipine/nicardipine

Why · Contractility support plus arterial unloading.

Failure · BP falls but CI does not rise.

Pivot · Check volume and obstruction; use flow/echo response.

07

Ischemia / arterial-graft concern

Combination · NTG + carefully titrated inotrope/pressor

Why · Coronary/conduit dilation with maintained flow and pressure.

Failure · Loss of preload/DBP or excessive α-tone.

Pivot · Balance CPP against graft-spasm risk.

08

Postoperative hypertension, preserved CI

Combination · Clevidipine or nicardipine; NTG if ischemic/preload phenotype

Why · Treats afterload without unnecessary inotropy.

Failure · Reflex tachycardia, negative inotropy or delayed nicardipine tail.

Pivot · Choose by target and desired offset; confirm CI remains adequate.

TRANSPARENT DEVELOPMENT

How this pilot teaching tool was developed

Bedside pattern recognition was translated into testable pathways—not presented as a settled universal protocol.

DEVELOPMENT SEQUENCE

  1. Begin with the CVICU “belt bag”: preload, contractility, afterload and obstruction.
  2. Map each medication to receptors, PK/PD, bedside effect and dominant liability.
  3. Extract complementary-pair logic from cardiac-surgery references, trials and labels.
  4. Separate established pharmacology, evidence-informed practice and proposed thresholds.
  5. Build phenotype-specific escalation and weaning scenarios.
  6. Pilot with CVICU nurses, APPs, pharmacists, fellows and intensivists.

EVIDENCE STATUS

Established Receptor pharmacology, short catecholamine offset, milrinone renal/long tail and vasodilator class effects.

Evidence-informed Complementary mechanisms and vasopressin after cardiac surgery.

Proposed pilot Exact ceilings, intervals, hold criteria and universalized sequence.

Requires validation A standardized milrinone–pressor bridge across phenotypes.
