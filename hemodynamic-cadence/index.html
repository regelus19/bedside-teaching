START HERE

Complete the cardiac-output picture before choosing a drug

Blood pressure is not the same as flow. Start with CO = HR × SV, then ask what is limiting heart rate/rhythm or stroke volume.

CARDIAC OUTPUT = HEART RATE / RHYTHM × STROKE VOLUME (preload · contractility · afterload)

HEART RATE & RHYTHM · How often and how efficiently?

Read: rate, rhythm, AV synchrony, pacing, filling time and stroke-volume response.

Too slow: output may be rate-limited. Too fast: filling and coronary perfusion time fall.

Remember: tachycardia may be harmful—or compensatory for low SV.

PRELOAD · How full is the tank?

Read: CVP, PA diastolic/PCWP, echo, passive-leg-raise and SV response.

Too little: low filling and low SV. Too much: congestion, dilation and ventricular interaction.

CONTRACTILITY · How hard does it squeeze?

Read: CI/SV, SvO₂, CPO, lactate pattern and echo.

Tools: dobutamine, low-dose epinephrine, milrinone, pacing and MCS evaluation.

AFTERLOAD · How tight is the nozzle?

Read: SVR/PVR, MAP/DBP and the ventricular response—not SVR alone.

Too loose: NE/vasopressin. Too tight: NTG/nicardipine/clevidipine when appropriate.

STOP-AND-DIAGNOSE GATE: Before stacking drugs, exclude bleeding, tamponade, tension physiology, ischemia, graft/valve obstruction, severe dyssynchrony and device failure.

SIX-QUESTION BEDSIDE SCAN

  1. Is CI/SV or CPO low—and is the measurement trustworthy?
  2. Is HR/rhythm helping or stealing filling time?
  3. Is preload inadequate, adequate, or congested?
  4. Is contractility failing despite adequate filling?
  5. Is systemic or pulmonary afterload mismatched?
  6. Could a mechanical problem explain non-response?

NEVER TITRATE TO MAP ALONE

  • Rate/rhythm: HR, AV synchrony, pacing and filling time
  • Flow: CI, SV, CPO and SvO₂
  • Coronary pressure: DBP and CPP
  • Congestion: CVP/PCWP and echo
  • Perfusion/toxicity: mentation, urine, skin, ectopy, ischemia and lactate pattern
ALGORITHM 1

Escalate by ceiling-and-pivot—not by maximizing one drug

Proposed pilot ranges require a current order set, clinician assessment and institutional policy.

LOW OUTPUT / STUNNED LV · CI <2.0, SvO₂ <60%, FILLING ADEQUATE/HIGH

1

Initial inotropy

Dobutamine 2–2.5 mcg/kg/min or epinephrine 0.01–0.02 mcg/kg/min. Reassess q15–20 or q10–15 min.

2

Ceiling

HR >95 or >10–15% above baseline, new ectopy/PVCs, dobutamine ~5, or epi 0.04–0.05 → cap drug A.

3

Complementary pivot

Consider milrinone 0.125–0.25 mcg/kg/min; omit loading bolus in unstable hypotension. Expect a 6–12 h build, longer in AKI.

DBP <50 or CPP <35: hold and verify with MD/APP; protect pressure with ordered NE and/or vasopressin.

POST-CPB VASOPLEGIA · MAP <65, SVR <800, CI PRESERVED/ADEQUATE

1

Adrenergic tone

NE 0.02–0.04 mcg/kg/min; reassess q5–10 min.

2

Ceiling pivot

NE ~0.08–0.10 or SVR >1100 with falling CI → cap NE; add vasopressin 0.03 U/min under active order.

3

Allow the slower component to declare itself

Assess the combined response over ~45–60 min before repeatedly stacking dose.

Refractory vasoplegia/acidosis: notify MD/APP for rescue evaluation.

RV FAILURE / HIGH PVR · RISING CVP, RV DILATION, PULMONARY HYPERTENSION

1

Correct PVR drivers

Hypoxia, acidosis, temperature, rhythm/AV synchrony and preload without RV overdistension.

2

Protect RV perfusion

Ordered NE ± low-dose vasopressin to preserve DBP/CPP.

3

Uncouple the RV

Milrinone 0.125–0.25 without bolus and/or inhaled pulmonary vasodilator under MD/APP direction.

AFTERLOAD EXCESS / HYPERTENSIVE LOW OUTPUT

1

Choose the vascular target

Nitroglycerin for preload/ischemia/conduit spasm; nicardipine or clevidipine for arterial afterload.

2

Pair when flow remains low

Combine afterload reduction with ordered dobutamine/milrinone rather than escalating catecholamine alone.

3

Match cadence

Clevidipine is minute-to-minute; NTG is rapid; nicardipine accumulates more slowly and has a longer tail. Reassess CI/SV—not BP alone.

ALGORITHM 2

PK-aware de-escalation: remove the liability, preserve the bridge

Enter only after a stable trend, correction of reversible causes, and exclusion of bleeding or mechanical failure.

1

Remove epinephrine liability

0.04 → 0.02 → 0.01 → off, approximately q15–20 min.

2

Trim norepinephrine to a low buffer

Reduce toward 0.02–0.04 mcg/kg/min, q10–15 min. HR/CI may fall as β₁ support disappears.

3

Test vasopressin while retaining a small NE rescue path

When NE ≤0.04: 0.03 → 0.02 → 0.01 → off, q45–60 min. Sequence is phenotype-dependent; comparative evidence is not universal.

4

Remove residual norepinephrine

0.02 → off, q10–15 min if DBP/CPP and forward flow remain stable.

5

Wean inotrope / inodilator

Dobutamine 5 → 2.5 → 1 → off, q20–30 min. Milrinone 0.25 → 0.1875 → 0.125 → off, generally q6–8 h; consider q12–24 h in significant AKI/CRRT.

THE MILRINONE–PRESSOR BRIDGE

  1. Reduce milrinone by the ordered step.
  2. Keep low NE and/or vasopressin during the residual vasodilatory tail.
  3. Wait hours—not minutes; longer with renal dysfunction.
  4. Reassess DBP/CPP, CI/SV, filling pressure, SvO₂ and perfusion.
  5. Only then test removal of the short-acting pressure bridge.

Inference: pharmacologically coherent and supported by milrinone PK/vasodilation, but the exact bridge sequence requires prospective validation.

HOLD WEANING; VERIFY WITH MD/APP

  • Meaningful DBP/MAP/CPP deterioration
  • CI/SV/CPO or SvO₂ deterioration from the stable trend
  • CVP/PCWP rise, RV dilation or pulmonary edema
  • New ischemia, tachyarrhythmia or ectopy
  • Unexplained lactate rise, oliguria, cool skin, altered mentation or acidosis

AFTERLOAD REDUCERS ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE

Nitroglycerin
Best for venous preload reduction, ischemia or conduit spasm; can collapse preload in an underfilled or RV-dependent patient.

Nicardipine
Sustained arterial unloading; avoid rapid stacking before its effect declares itself.

Clevidipine
Ultra-short arterial control when frequent fine adjustment and reversibility matter.

PATTERN RECOGNITION

Combination scenarios: why they fit, how they fail, what to reassess

Teaching scenarios—not prescriptive orders.

01

Stunned LV + vasodilation

Combination · Dobutamine or low-dose epi + NE

Why · Flow support plus DBP/CPP protection.

Failure · Tachycardia or rising afterload with falling SV.

Pivot · Cap catecholamine; consider milrinone while preserving a pressure bridge.

02

Tachycardia-limited inotropy

Combination · Milrinone + low-dose NE or vasopressin

Why · Non-β inotropy/lusitropy while countering dilation.

Failure · Pressor removed before milrinone is reduced or cleared.

Pivot · Use the PK bridge; wait longer in AKI.

03

Mixed cardiogenic–vasoplegic shock

Combination · Inotrope/inodilator + NE + vasopressin

Why · Separates flow from tone and limits single-drug dose.

Failure · MAP improves while CI/SV falls from excess afterload.

Pivot · Trend CPO, SvO₂ and filling pressure with SVR.

04

Isolated RV failure / high PVR

Combination · Milrinone + NE ± vasopressin; inhaled pulmonary vasodilator

Why · Reduces PVR while preserving right-coronary pressure.

Failure · Systemic pressure loss or RV overdistension.

Pivot · Protect DBP; correct hypoxia, acidosis and rhythm.

05

Post-CPB vasoplegia with adequate CI

Combination · NE → add vasopressin

Why · Adrenergic plus V1a tone; catecholamine-sparing.

Failure · Persistent acidosis or rising SVR with declining CI.

Pivot · Stop stacking and notify for rescue evaluation.

06

Hypertensive low-output LV

Combination · Dobutamine/milrinone + clevidipine/nicardipine

Why · Contractility support plus arterial unloading.

Failure · BP falls but CI does not rise.

Pivot · Check volume and obstruction; use flow/echo response.

07

Ischemia / arterial-graft concern

Combination · NTG + carefully titrated inotrope/pressor

Why · Coronary/conduit dilation with maintained flow and pressure.

Failure · Loss of preload/DBP or excessive α-tone.

Pivot · Balance CPP against graft-spasm risk.

08

Postoperative hypertension, preserved CI

Combination · Clevidipine or nicardipine; NTG if ischemic/preload phenotype

Why · Treats afterload without unnecessary inotropy.

Failure · Reflex tachycardia, negative inotropy or delayed nicardipine tail.

Pivot · Choose by target and desired offset; confirm CI remains adequate.

TRANSPARENT DEVELOPMENT

How this pilot teaching tool was developed

Bedside pattern recognition was translated into testable pathways—not presented as a settled universal protocol.

DEVELOPMENT SEQUENCE

  1. Begin with the CVICU “belt bag”: preload, contractility, afterload and obstruction.
  2. Map each medication to receptors, PK/PD, bedside effect and dominant liability.
  3. Extract complementary-pair logic from cardiac-surgery references, trials and labels.
  4. Separate established pharmacology, evidence-informed practice and proposed thresholds.
  5. Build phenotype-specific escalation and weaning scenarios.
  6. Pilot with CVICU nurses, APPs, pharmacists, fellows and intensivists.

EVIDENCE STATUS

Established Receptor pharmacology, short catecholamine offset, milrinone renal/long tail and vasodilator class effects.

Requires validation A standardized milrinone–pressor bridge across phenotypes.

PRECEPTOR COMPENDIUM

Clinical gems and bedside mantras

High-yield teaching distilled from Bojar, Kathy White and contemporary pharmacology. Pearls guide reassessment; they do not replace orders or current guidelines.

Historical-source caution: Kathy White's 2011 “Words of Advice” are valuable teaching prompts, but drug claims and absolute wording must be checked against current labeling, guidelines and local practice.

“Blood pressure is not cardiac output.”

A high SVR can preserve MAP while stroke volume and tissue flow remain poor. Read pressure and flow together.

Bojar · Framework
“Rate is part of output—but every beat needs time to fill.”

Because CO = HR × SV, slowing a compensatory rhythm can collapse output; excessive tachycardia can shorten filling and coronary perfusion.

Bojar · White
“Do not vasodilate an empty tank.”

Confirm preload and exclude tamponade or fixed obstruction before adding an inodilator or arterial vasodilator to hypotension.

White · bedside pearl
“Do not mistake hypertension for recovery.”

Before stopping inotropic support, confirm that CI/SV is satisfactory and not merely being masked by vasoconstriction.

Bojar · White
“When drug A reaches its liability, add a complementary drug B.”

Multi-agent support can reduce the adverse effects of driving one receptor pathway to higher doses.

Bojar · mechanism-based inference
“Levophed is not just squeeze.”

Norepinephrine also supplies β₁ support; HR and CI may fall when it is removed even if MAP initially looks acceptable.

Bojar
“Milrinone leaves slowly; the pressure bridge leaves fast.”

Its vasodilatory effect may outlast NE. Reduce milrinone, wait through its residual effect—longer in renal dysfunction—then test the pressor.

DailyMed · PK-informed practice
“Protect diastole.”

CPP depends on aortic diastolic pressure minus ventricular filling pressure. A tolerable MAP can hide inadequate coronary driving pressure.

Bojar · White
“The RV needs pressure—but not indiscriminate volume.”

Maintain systemic/diastolic pressure, correct hypoxia and acidosis, preserve AV synchrony and avoid RV overdistension.

Bojar
“Catecholamines buy flow with oxygen.”

Epinephrine and dobutamine can raise myocardial oxygen demand, heart rate and arrhythmia risk. Trend benefit against liability.

Bojar · OPTIMA-CC
“The first 4–6 hours may be the nadir; persistence changes the question.”

Transient post-CPB depression is expected. Continued low output, rising filling pressures, oliguria, acidosis or ischemia should trigger echo and MCS evaluation.

Bojar
“Arterial grafts dislike excessive alpha tone.”

Pure or high-dose vasoconstriction may compromise ITA/radial conduit flow; balance perfusion pressure against afterload and graft-spasm risk.

Bojar
“A suddenly quiet chest tube is not always good news.”

When brisk drainage stops abruptly and hemodynamics worsen, investigate clot obstruction and tamponade rather than celebrating the number.

White · urgent diagnostic pearl
“Extubation is a stress test.”

Spontaneous breathing increases work, HR and oxygen demand; watch for ischemia and hemodynamic intolerance during and after liberation.

White
“Activity unmasks limited reserve.”

Anemia and low output may declare themselves on first mobilization as rising HR, falling BP or SvO₂ and poor tolerance.

REFERENCE BANK

Plain-language glossary

Use this tab whenever a bedside term is unfamiliar. Linked terms throughout the module open here.

Hemodynamic pattern (phenotype)

The recognizable circulation problem shown by the patient's data—for example low flow, low vascular tone, RV failure, excess afterload or a mixed state.

Preload

The filling/stretch present before contraction; estimated from history, exam, filling pressures, echo and response to a dynamic fluid test.

Contractility

The heart muscle's intrinsic squeezing strength, separate from preload and afterload.

Afterload

The resistance or pressure the ventricle must overcome to eject blood. The LV faces systemic afterload; the RV faces pulmonary afterload.

Stroke volume (SV)

The amount of blood ejected with each beat. Preload, contractility and afterload all influence it.

Cardiac output (CO)

Blood pumped per minute: heart rate × stroke volume.

Cardiac index (CI)

Cardiac output adjusted for body surface area, allowing comparison across body sizes.

AV synchrony

Coordinated atrial contraction followed by ventricular contraction. Losing the atrial contribution can markedly reduce filling in a stiff ventricle.

Lusitropy

Myocardial relaxation. Better relaxation can improve ventricular filling but may accompany systemic vasodilation with milrinone.

Inotrope

A medication that increases contractile force, such as dobutamine or epinephrine.

Inodilator

A drug that increases contractility while lowering vascular resistance, such as milrinone.

Vasopressor

A medication that raises vascular tone and blood pressure, such as norepinephrine or vasopressin.

SVR / PVR

Systemic/pulmonary vascular resistance: calculated estimates of LV/RV vascular afterload.

CPP

Coronary perfusion pressure: approximated as aortic diastolic pressure minus ventricular end-diastolic pressure.

CPO

Cardiac power output: a combined pressure-and-flow measure, calculated as MAP × CO ÷ 451.

SvO₂

Mixed venous oxygen saturation: the oxygen remaining after tissues extract what they need; interpreted as a trend with flow, hemoglobin and oxygen demand.

Vasoplegia

Pathologically low vascular tone causing hypotension despite adequate or high cardiac output, often after CPB.

Ventriculo-arterial coupling

How well ventricular contraction is matched to the resistance of the arterial system. Excess afterload can waste cardiac work and lower SV.

Type B epinephrine lactate

Lactate elevation from β₂-driven accelerated glycolysis rather than inadequate tissue oxygen delivery. It remains a diagnosis of context, not an automatic reassurance.

Pharmacokinetic cadence

The reassessment interval chosen to respect a drug's onset, distribution, steady-state behavior and elimination.

Ceiling-and-pivot

Stop escalating a drug when its adverse-effect threshold is reached, then add or switch to a complementary mechanism.

Pressure bridge

Shorter-acting vasopressor support temporarily maintained while a longer-acting vasodilating drug is reduced and clears.

MCS

Mechanical circulatory support, such as IABP or temporary ventricular assist devices, considered when pharmacologic support is insufficient.

CPB

Cardiopulmonary bypass: the heart-lung machine used during cardiac surgery.
